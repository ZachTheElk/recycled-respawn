The feeling that I keep having while playing Zero Company is that there is an actual sense of urgency, while at the same time a relaxed feeling and a sense of home and belonging. Two sides to a very dynamic coin, the tactical combat of the game is a direct representation of one of those sides of that coin. Choosing a mission at the war table, jumping in the ship, heading towards the planet, jumping out of the ship, hitting the ground under fire, all of those things invoke a sense of urgency and a requirement/responsibility almost that immediate action is needed. Conversely, the very calm, chill, relaxed vibe of The Den with all my ne'er-do-well companions, is a place I find myself spending more time than may or may not be intended. Star Wars Zero Company shines by giving you just enough player agency with the right amount of direction and pace.

The game really provides a surprising amount of player freedom and choice, even so far as the ability to basically act or move, let someone else do the same, and then do it again on the first character, multiple that by the amount of party members you have, and you have quite a lot of potential. It's a surprising mix of tactical/turn based combat with a sense and feel of real time action and thought process. It's not just a decision to decide to move here, shoot at an enemy, press confirm and hope that is a good decision and works out. The ability to sort of adapt on the fly as combat is happening alongside of you really increases my personal investment and sense of "this is me as my character."

The timer does make me anxious because its a sort of deadline that I have been suddenly faced with, I'm not annoyed by it in that same aspect because I am forced to act, I have to save that person because if not they are going to do, we can decide if it was annoying later. I think that it does limit and possibly recategorize the list of possibilities that I have in the moment mentally, but again not in a bad way, it is just forcing me to maybe not immediately right now this turn have to deal with something, but consider in as something inevitable in the very near future. It makes success feel incredibly more dramatic, the amount of times after a successful extraction/end of mission that I have said "by the skin of my teeth." an audible reaction is noteworthy, at least for me.

I think a lot of that illusion is created and sustained by the source material (Star Wars) itself, imagining a dropship swooping in to pick up my team isn't that different/draws direct comparison from the movies/tv. A turn based game evokes something kinetic I think when there's a specific deliberation then action phase, you are already having to imagine what might happen if I do what I'm about to do, to me it makes sense for an action sequence to already be sort of playing in your head.

I already prioritize B2s when they prepare rockets, so making them harder to kill may be worse for the way I personally play than simply increasing the rocket threat, my thought process behind this is the threat of a stronger/more deadly rocket impact at first scared me more than the +2 defense, I thought to get rid of the rocket damage upgrade over the defensive upgrade because in my mind bigger threat. But I thought about how I approach the battle/engagements with the B2 specifically, and I always prioritize them, therefore, ensuring that they dont have any more defenses or health, was actually the right choice, in my opinion. Now if they do arm their stronger rockets, I will either prioritize them even heavier than I am already, or get further enough away.
